Dear Hiring Manager,

Over the years my **passion for 3D printers and manufacturing** has grown from drooling over newly-attainable FDM 3D printers when I was in high school, to building my own FDM printer, to **<a href='https://www.nathankadria.com/projects/capstone'>designing and building a novel format of wire EDM machine</a>** to cut DED printed parts off of build plates. I’m constantly learning everything I can about additive (and subtractive) manufacturing. I dream of one day owning a shop filled with every kind of machine I can imagine, or working somewhere that lets me use theirs!
>With my experience leading the design and production of automation systems in fast-paced startups using SolidWorks and Onshape,
>mastery of the iterative design process using 3D printed and sheet metal parts, and
>passion for all kinds of manufacturing,
>I’m confident I would be a great fit as a **Printer Research Engineer at Quadratic 3D.**

In my co-op experiences at fast-paced startups, **I took ownership of projects while collaborating across teams.** Most recently, I led the mechanical design of **<a href='https://www.nathankadria.com/projects/greensight'>WeatherHive</a>**, an autonomous drone silo system capable of storing, charging, launching, and loading 10 weather drones, and led the production of 6 of these systems, all in just a few months. **I used designed 3D printed, sheet metal, and machined parts** and carefully selected COTS parts from vendors. I avoided complexity and machined parts wherever possible, but didn’t hesitate to take matters into my own hands and machine/fabricate a part myself when necessary. Throughout the design and production process **I coordinated constantly with other engineering teams,** making dummy systems for them to test with to allow for parallel development and seamless integration down the line. **I conducted subsystem testing and validation before scaling up production,** and iterated on designs to ensure the Hive was reliable and effective. WeatherHive met the requirements for the initial customer, and is a **successful product that continues to be marketed to new customers.**
>I took pride in my work on WeatherHive, and strove to design an elegant system that was as simple as it was effective.

My engineering capstone project also gave me **experience working with novel manufacturing processes.** In order to have any chance of breaking into a market with well established players like Mitsubishi, FANUC, and Makino, we had to address a niche in the market at a fraction of the cost.  My group designed a machine to cut metal 3D printed parts (DED) off of build plates for an aerospace client. These parts are much larger than the working areas of most WEDM machines, but require much less precision. 
>Within a few months, my group designed, built, and delivered a WEDM machine that satisfied the unique requirements of our client.

I am excited by the possibility of working with Quadratic 3D to push the boundaries of 3D printing. Thank you for your time and consideration.
